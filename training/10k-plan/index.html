10K Training Plan

A 10K training plan is a structured schedule that builds the endurance to cover 10 kilometres (6.2 miles) and the speed to race it. Rutz is an AI running coach app for iPhone that writes your 10K plan week by week, based on how your last week actually went.

How to train for a 10K

The 10K is the honest distance: too long to fake on speed, too short to coast on endurance. You need both. That means a weekly long run, one threshold or tempo session, and a lot of easy running between them.

Build your long run gradually toward 12-16 km so that race distance feels short. Tempo efforts — 20 to 30 minutes at a comfortably hard pace — are the single most useful 10K workout.

If you're stepping up from 5K, keep the speedwork you know and simply add distance. Your weekly volume grows by a little at a time; the plan holds you back when your load climbs too fast.

Key sessions

The workouts that matter for a 10k

Every session in your plan has a job. These four do the heavy lifting at this distance.

Cruise intervals

Four to six reps of 1 km or 5 minutes at threshold effort with a short jog between. More time at 10K-relevant intensity than one continuous tempo.

Long run to 12-16 km

Going past race distance in training is what makes 10 km feel short on the day. All easy, no heroics.

Progression run

Start easy and finish the last third at 10K effort. Trains you to run fast on tired legs — the final 3 km of the race.

Strides

Six 20-second pick-ups after an easy run. Keeps your 5K speed alive while the distance grows.

10K training tips

Pacing

Even splits win 10Ks. Settle in for 3 km, work through 7, race the last 3.

Long run

Going past race distance in training is the quickest way to make 10 km feel manageable.

Strength

Hips and calves carry you through the back half. Two short sessions a week is plenty.

Fuelling

You won't need gels for a 10K, but you do need to start hydrated and fed. Practise your pre-race breakfast.

10K training: your questions, answered

How long is a 10K?

A 10K is 10 kilometres, or 6.2 miles — 25 laps of a standard track.

How long does it take to train for a 10K?

If you can already run 5K, 8 to 10 weeks is enough. From a standing start, allow 12 weeks or more.

I can run 5K. How do I get to 10K?

Keep most runs easy, add about 1 km to your long run each week, and take an easier week every third or fourth week. Once your long run reaches 8 km, 10K is within reach.

What is a good 10K time?

Many recreational runners finish between 55 and 70 minutes. Under 50 minutes is a solid milestone, and under 40 takes consistent structured training.

How many days a week should I run for a 10K?

Three to four days works for finishing comfortably. Four to five, including a long run and one tempo or interval session, suits runners chasing a time.

What pace is a 50 minute 10K?

A 50:00 10K is 5:00 per kilometre, or 8:03 per mile. The Rutz pace chart for 10K lists every finish time from 35 to 80 minutes.
