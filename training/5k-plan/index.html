5K Training Plan

A 5K training plan is a week-by-week schedule of easy runs, one faster session and rest that prepares you to run 5 kilometres (3.1 miles). Rutz is an AI running coach app for iPhone that writes your 5K plan one week at a time, from the runs you actually did.

How to train for a 5K

The 5K rewards consistency more than heroics. Three runs a week is enough to finish your first; four or five is enough to get properly fast. Most of that running should feel easy — easy enough to hold a conversation.

Add one session a week that isn't easy: short intervals, a tempo effort or hills. That single hard day is where speed comes from. Everything else is there so you can absorb it.

New to running? Start with run-walk intervals and stretch the running parts a little each week. There is no shame in walking. It is the fastest route to running the whole way.

Key sessions

The workouts that matter for a 5k

Every session in your plan has a job. These four do the heavy lifting at this distance.

Run-walk intervals

For first-timers: one minute running, two walking, repeated. The running parts stretch a little each week until the walks disappear.

400 m repeats

Eight to twelve laps of the track at goal 5K pace or a touch quicker, with an equal jog between. The classic 5K sharpener.

20-minute tempo

Comfortably hard and continuous. It teaches you to sit at an uncomfortable pace without panicking — most of what a 5K is.

Easy 30-40 minutes

The run that makes the fast ones possible. Two or three of these a week, slow enough to chat.

5K training tips

Pacing

Go out at your goal pace, not faster. The first kilometre always feels free. You pay for it in the fourth.

Warm up

The shorter the race, the longer the warm-up. Ten easy minutes and a few strides before a hard 5K.

Strength

Two short sessions a week — calves, glutes, single-leg work — keeps niggles from becoming injuries.

Recovery

Fitness is built on the easy days. Sleep is training. Protect both.

5K training: your questions, answered

How long is a 5K?

A 5K is 5 kilometres, or 3.1 miles. On a standard 400 m track that is twelve and a half laps.

How long does it take to train for a 5K?

Most new runners can go from nothing to a continuous 5K in 8 to 12 weeks on three runs a week. If you already run regularly, 6 to 8 weeks of focused training is enough to race one well.

I'm a complete beginner. Can I really run a 5K?

Yes. Start with run-walk intervals — for example one minute running, two minutes walking — and lengthen the running a little each week. Nearly everyone who stays consistent for 8 to 12 weeks gets there.

What is a good 5K time?

It depends on your age and background. Many first-timers finish in 30 to 40 minutes. Under 25 minutes is a strong club-level time, and under 20 minutes takes structured training. The only time that matters is faster than your last one.

How many days a week should I run for a 5K?

Three days is enough to finish comfortably. Four to five days, with one faster session, is where most runners see their biggest improvements.

What pace should I run my 5K at?

Divide your goal time by five for pace per kilometre. A 25:00 5K is 5:00 per km, or 8:03 per mile. Use the Rutz pace calculator to work out yours.
